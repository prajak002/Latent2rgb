—Contribution

  • A protocol for a question prior methods can't ask. Representation-inversion methods (Mahendran & Vedaldi, Nash et al., FAE, DPS, SPADE, Neuralangelo, GeoDE) invert the latent of an image that already exists. This inverts a rolled-forward latent, for a frame that was never observed, with a decoder kept deliberately too weak to hallucinate — so the output is evidence about the latent, not about the decoder.
  • A measurement-gap finding, with confidence intervals, not bare point estimates. Section 8 below shows pixel_error moving several times more than latent_drift under horizon. Section 10 shows that six candidate latent-only proxies for that divergence — including an entropy-rate metric ported from ecological crash detection — all fail to substitute for it: weak correlation whose 95% CIs include zero for all six candidates, zero early-warning lead time (Wilson 95% CI [0%, 32%]), a sign flip across CIs that don't overlap between an unrelated pipeline choice, and a pairwise condition ranking that's mostly intransitive (10 explicit 3-cycles — a formal violation, not a sampling artifact).
  • Per-clip evidence. The rollout gallery shows this isn't an averaged artifact: six real clips, each annotated with its own latent_drift/pixel_error range, and the divergence ratio between them varies clip to clip — consistent with why no single latent-space threshold generalizes.
  • A second, differently-trained predictor — and the gap changes shape. Section 13 runs the identical protocol against a frozen DINOv2 encoder paired with a small predictor trained from scratch on the same clips. The gap doesn't just shrink or vanish: its direction inverts (latent_drift moves more than pixel_error here, not less), suggesting the gap tracks frozen, off-distribution querying specifically — not latent-only prediction as a category.

1.Research question

Given a frozen, latent-only state predictor P (no native pixel decoder) and its rolled-forward latent ẑ[t+k] = P(z[t], k): does decoding ẑ[t+k] reveal a pixel-space error that diverges from how far ẑ[t+k] sits from the truth in representation space? And does a decode error, once re-injected into the rollout, compound as k grows?

No prior representation-inversion method can ask this — each inverts a latent of an image that already exists. A rolled-forward latent represents a future that never happened.

2.Proposed solution

  • Instrument. Attach a deliberately minimal decoder (single linear layer per token, plain pixel loss, no perceptual/adversarial/diffusion loss) to a predictor that genuinely ships no decoder (V-JEPA2). A stronger decoder would hallucinate detail the latent doesn't carry, hiding the failure being measured.
  • Validation. Before trusting any result, check the instrument itself isn't the source of the signal — does the decoder leak context or snap to a generic output.
  • Compounding test. Decode a rolled-forward latent, re-encode it, re-inject it into the rollout, and check whether the resulting error grows or shrinks with further horizon.

4.Experimentation methodology

  1. Context: encode real frames [0..t] with the frozen V-JEPA2 ViT-L encoder → token sequence, 256 tokens per 2-frame tubelet.
  2. Causal query: construct masks_x (context) / masks_y (target tubelet at t+k), one predictor call per k ∈ {2,4,8,16,32}. Out-of-distribution for the predictor — it was trained to fill gaps in an already-observed clip, never to extrapolate a causal past into an unseen future.
  3. Decode: the linear decoder turns predicted tokens back into 2 RGB frames.
  4. Compare: latent_drift, pixel_error, and pixel_error read against the decoder's own real-frame reconstruction floor.
  5. Honesty checks: context leakage (decode clip A's latent, compare to clip A vs. clip B) and snapping (interpolate between two real clips' latents, check for smooth vs. jump degradation) — on real encoded latents, no predictor involved.
  6. Re-injection: decode + re-encode the predicted tubelet at k=8, extend the context two ways (raw vs. round-tripped), query j ∈ {2,4} further, compare.
  7. Cross-check: every step repeated independently on both datasets.

Not run: counterfactual faithfulness (rolling one state under two different actions). The base predictor has no action input — this needs V-JEPA2-AC and a real action-conditioned dataset, not more compute on this setup.

6.System design

One pass of the protocol on one (clip, start $t$, horizon $k$) triple. The solid path is the measurement; the dashed paths are the two things that make the measurement admissible — the decoder-honesty gate (S8) and the floor (S6b) — plus the re-injection loop (S9).

S1Causal context window

Take the largest whole number of tubelets that ends at or before frame $t$. Nothing at or after $t+1$ may enter.

$$L_t = \tau\left\lfloor \tfrac{t+1}{\tau} \right\rfloor, \qquad Z_t = E_\theta\!\left(x_{0:L_t}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{ctx}}\times D}, \qquad N_{\text{ctx}} = \tfrac{L_t}{\tau}\,n$$
requires L_t ≥ τ — at least one full tubelet of context
in clip $x$, start $t$  ·  out $Z_t$  ·  impl latent2rgb/data.py · RolloutBuilder.build
S2Target slot resolution

Horizon $k$ is expressed in raw frames, but the smallest addressable unit is a tubelet. $k$ resolves to the slot containing frame $t+k$, and the later frame of that pair is the ground-truth frame compared against.

$$s_k = \left\lfloor \tfrac{t+k}{\tau} \right\rfloor, \qquad x^\star_k = x_{\tau s_k + 1}, \qquad \text{valid iff}\quad \underbrace{s_k \ \ge\ \tfrac{L_t}{\tau}}_{\text{no overlap with context}} \ \ \wedge\ \ \underbrace{s_k < 32}_{\text{predictor grid depth}}$$
the left condition is why k=1 is dropped: from an even t it would target a slot already inside the context
out $s_k$, $x^\star_k$  ·  impl latent2rgb/data.py, vjepa_adapter.py · rollout
S3Masked causal query — the out-of-distribution step

The predictor is addressed by two index sets into the flattened token grid: $M_x$ gathers the context it is given, $M_y$ names the positions it must fill.

$$M_x = \{0,\dots,N_{\text{ctx}}-1\}, \qquad M_y = \{\,n s_k,\ \dots,\ n s_k + n - 1\,\}$$ $$\hat{Z}_k \;=\; P_\phi\!\left(Z_t;\, M_x,\, M_y\right) \ \in\ \mathbb{R}^{n\times D}$$

This is one forward pass, not a $k$-step loop: the predictor answers what is at this position given context. The regime is what makes it out-of-distribution, and it can be stated exactly. Let $q_{\text{train}}$ be the mask distribution V-JEPA2 was pretrained under — context and target both sampled across an already-observed clip. Then the masks used here are a null event under it:

$$\Pr_{(M_x,M_y)\sim q_{\text{train}}}\Big[\ \max_{i \in M_x}\big\lfloor i/n \big\rfloor \;<\; \min_{j \in M_y}\big\lfloor j/n \big\rfloor \ \Big] \;=\; 0$$
strict temporal separation — every context token precedes every target token. Architecturally constructible, never trained. Every number downstream characterises the frozen predictor under this query, not its native task.
in $Z_t$, $s_k$  ·  out $\hat Z_k$  ·  impl latent2rgb/vjepa_adapter.py · VJEPA2Predictor.rollout
S4Ground-truth latent

The same encoder, applied to the real tubelet the rollout claims to have predicted.

$$Z^\star_k \;=\; E_\theta\!\left(x_{\tau s_k\,:\,\tau s_k + \tau}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times D}$$
valid to compare against Ẑ_k because the predictor's out_embed_dim defaults to the encoder's D — both live in the same space
out $Z^\star_k$  ·  impl latent2rgb/data.py
S5Decode — the instrument

A single linear map per token, a sigmoid, and the exact inverse of the encoder's patch-flatten. No cross-token mixing anywhere in it.

$$\big[D_\psi(\hat Z)\big]_{\text{patch } j} \;=\; \operatorname{fold}\Big(\sigma\big(W \hat z_j + b\big)\Big), \qquad W \in \mathbb{R}^{(C\tau p^2)\times D},\ \ C\tau p^2 = 1536$$
patch j of the output depends on token j and on nothing else · representative decoded frame: x̂_k = D(Ẑ_k)[:, τ−1]

That factorisation is the whole honesty argument, and §11.4 states it as a constraint rather than a hope.

in $\hat Z_k$  ·  out $\hat x_k$  ·  impl latent2rgb/decoder.py · MinimalDecoder
S6The two error coordinates, the floor, and the ratio

Representation-space and pixel-space error, each measured against the truth, plus the decoder's own reconstruction floor on real (never rolled-forward) tubelets.

$$\delta_k \;=\; \big\|\hat Z_k - Z^\star_k\big\|_F \;=\; \sqrt{\textstyle\sum_{i,d}\big(\hat Z_{k,id}-Z^\star_{k,id}\big)^2}$$ $$\varepsilon_k \;=\; \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{CHW}\textstyle\sum\big(\hat x_k - x^\star_k\big)^2}, \qquad \varepsilon_{\text{floor}} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\text{real}}\Big[\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{C\tau HW}\textstyle\sum\big(D_\psi(E_\theta(x)) - x\big)^2}\Big]$$ $$\operatorname{excess}_k \;=\; \varepsilon_k - \varepsilon_{\text{floor}}$$

$\delta$ is an unnormalised Frobenius norm over $256\times1024$ entries; $\varepsilon$ is a per-pixel RMSE in $[0,1]$. They are not comparable in magnitude and the protocol never compares them that way. What is compared is each one's dimensionless relative range over horizon, and their ratio — which is invariant to rescaling either coordinate:

$$R[u] \;=\; \frac{\max_k \bar u_k - \min_k \bar u_k}{\operatorname{mean}_k \bar u_k}, \qquad \rho \;=\; \frac{R[\varepsilon]}{R[\delta]}, \qquad R[cu]=R[u]\ \ \forall c>0$$
ρ > 1 ⟺ pixel error is the more horizon-sensitive coordinate. ρ < 1 ⟺ latent drift is. Scale-invariance is what makes the comparison legitimate at all.
out $\delta_k,\ \varepsilon_k,\ \rho$  ·  impl latent2rgb/metrics.py, scripts/stats_significance.py
S6cSeparation statistic — a projection residual

Fit pixel error against latent drift using only short horizons, then ask how far the long-horizon observation falls from that calibrated line.

$$(a,b) \;=\; \arg\min_{a,b}\ \sum_{k \in K_{\text{fit}}} \big(\varepsilon_k - a\,\delta_k - b\big)^2, \qquad r \;=\; \bar\varepsilon_{k^\ast} - \big(a\,\bar\delta_{k^\ast} + b\big)$$
r is the component of long-horizon pixel error orthogonal to the affine span of latent drift as calibrated at short horizon

$r \approx 0$ is the protocol's own stop condition: pixel-space evaluation would be adding nothing that latent-space evaluation didn't already say. $r$ large is the reason the protocol exists. Measured residuals: 0.0099 (V-JEPA2), 0.0004 (Family B), 0.0131 (Family C) — and the SIGReg note in Section 13 makes the point that residual size and the direction of $\rho$ are different axes.

impl latent2rgb/metrics.py · fit_separation_statistic
S8Decoder-honesty gate — runs before S6 is believed

Two checks on real encoded latents, no predictor involved. If either fails, every $\varepsilon$ above is measuring the instrument.

$$\Gamma \;=\; \big\|D_\psi(E_\theta(x_A)) - x_B\big\| \;-\; \big\|D_\psi(E_\theta(x_A)) - x_A\big\| \qquad \text{(context leakage; require } \Gamma \gg 0)$$ $$e(\alpha) = \big\|D_\psi\big((1-\alpha) z_A + \alpha z_B\big) - x_A\big\|, \quad \alpha_i = \tfrac{i}{n_s-1}, \qquad \kappa = \max_i \big| e_{i+1} - 2 e_i + e_{i-1} \big|$$
κ is a discrete second difference, κ ≈ h²·|e″|: a decoder that snaps to one plausible real frame shows a plateau then a spike. Both checks pass on real latents, on both datasets.
impl latent2rgb/instruments.py, scripts/stage_e_real.py
S9Re-injection — is a decode error a one-off or does it compound?

Round-trip a predicted latent through pixels once, resume the rollout from it, and compare against the path that was never decoded.

$$\Pi \;=\; E_\theta \circ D_\psi, \qquad Z' = \Pi(\hat Z_{k_r}), \qquad \eta = Z' - \hat Z_{k_r}$$ $$d_j \;=\; \big\| P_\phi(\hat Z_{k_r}, j) - P_\phi(Z', j) \big\|, \qquad j \in \{2,4\}$$

To first order in $\eta$, $d_j \approx \big\|J_{P_j}(\hat Z_{k_r})\,\eta\big\|$, so the slope of $d_j$ in $j$ is a finite-perturbation growth rate along the rollout — the empirical analogue of a Lyapunov exponent for the round-trip perturbation. Sign is the whole reading:

$$\text{rate} \;=\; \frac{\operatorname{Cov}_j(j,\, d_j)}{\operatorname{Var}_j(j)} \qquad \begin{cases} > 0 & \text{decode error compounds through the dynamics}\\ < 0 & \text{it contracts}\end{cases}$$
measured: −0.0248 on SSv2 — contraction, on both datasets. Consistent with a predictor largely insensitive to context under this OOD query: a perturbation to its input barely propagates.
impl latent2rgb/instruments.py · reinjection_divergence, scripts/stage_f_reinjection.py

7.The proxy battery (S7)

The obvious objection to S5–S6 is that decoding is expensive and maybe unnecessary — surely some cheap latent-only scalar tracks pixel divergence. Seven were defined and tested. Each is a functional $g$ producing one number per $(\text{clip}, k)$ row, to be correlated against $\varepsilon_k$.

Four of them need a perturbation ensemble. Member $0$ is always the unperturbed rollout:

$$Z^{(m)}_{\text{ctx}} = Z_t + \epsilon\,\hat\sigma(Z_t)\,\xi^{(m)},\quad \xi^{(m)}\!\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),\ m=1..M{-}1; \qquad \hat Z^{(m)}_k = P_\phi\big(Z^{(m)}_{\text{ctx}}, k\big)$$
M = 6, ε = 0.05, σ̂ = the empirical std of the context tokens — so the perturbation is scale-relative

Distance-type

$$g_1 = \big\|\hat Z_k - Z^\star_k\big\|_F \qquad g_2 = 1 - \frac{\langle \hat Z_k,\, Z^\star_k\rangle}{\|\hat Z_k\|\,\|Z^\star_k\|} \qquad g_3 = \frac{\|\hat Z_k - Z^\star_k\|}{\|Z^\star_k\|}$$
raw L2 drift · cosine distance · normalized L2 (relative error)

Spread-type, over the ensemble $X \in \mathbb{R}^{M\times nD}$ (flattened members)

$$g_4 \;=\; \frac{1}{nD}\sum_{i} \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}_m\big[X_{mi}\big] \qquad\text{(ensemble variance, unbiased in } m)$$ $$\tilde X = X - \tfrac{1}{M}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top X = U S V^\top,\qquad p_i = \frac{s_i^2}{\sum_j s_j^2},\qquad g_7 = H = -\sum_i p_i \ln p_i,\qquad g_5 = e^{H}$$
H is the spectral (von-Neumann-style) entropy of the ensemble's own covariance spectrum in nats; g₅ = e^H is effective rank (Roy & Vetterli). Bounded above by ln(min(M,nD) − 1).
$$\mu = \tfrac{1}{M}\textstyle\sum_m X_m, \quad \lambda_i = \tfrac{s_i^2}{M-1}, \qquad g_6 = d_M\big(Z^\star_k\big) = \sqrt{\ \sum_i \frac{\big\langle v_i,\ Z^\star_k - \mu\big\rangle^2}{\lambda_i + \varepsilon_{\!\text{num}}}\ }$$
Mahalanobis distance of the truth from the ensemble's own spread, computed through the economy SVD rather than by inverting a singular nD × nD covariance

The transplanted one: EBID entropy rate

$g_7$'s intended use is not as a static scalar but as a rate. Ported from ecological crash detection, where a falling entropy rate precedes population collapse:

$$\dot H(k) = \frac{dH}{dk}\ \ \text{(central differences, then a width-3 moving average)}, \qquad \theta = \min\Big(0,\ Q_{0.10}\big(\dot H\big)\Big)$$ $$k_{\text{warn}} = \min\Big\{ k_i \ :\ \dot H(k_{i'}) < \theta \ \ \text{for } c \text{ consecutive } i' \ge i \Big\},\quad c = 2$$ $$k_{\text{crash}} = \min\big\{k : \varepsilon_k \ge Q_{0.75}(\varepsilon)\big\}, \qquad \text{lead} = k_{\text{crash}} - k_{\text{warn}}$$
a usable early-warning signal needs lead > 0. Measured: 0 of 8 real crashes across 24 clips.

10.Measurement gap: does any latent-only proxy predict this?

Section 8 shows pixel_error diverging from latent_drift under horizon. The obvious follow-up: is there a cheap, latent-only signal that predicts that divergence itself, so a system without a decoder could still tell when its rollout is failing in pixel space? Experiments 1–6 stress-test six candidates — cosine distance, Mahalanobis distance, ensemble variance, effective rank, normalized L2 drift, and an entropy-rate metric (EBID) ported from ecological crash-detection — against real pixel divergence on real clips. All six fail, each in a different way:

1. Correlation — honestly bounded. Best candidate (normalized L2) reaches Pearson r = 0.12 vs. pixel_error; raw latent drift reaches r = 0.013. Cluster-bootstrapped 95% CIs (resampled by clip, n=24) include zero for all six, but at n=24 that alone only has ~80% power to detect |r|≈0.55 — CI upper bounds range from ~0.12 (Mahalanobis, tightest) to ~0.50 (ensemble variance, loosest), read per-candidate, not as one number. (scripts/experiment1_ebid_vs_baselines.py, scripts/stats_significance.py)

1b. Conformal efficiency — a power-independent check that agrees. Correlation-test power is a real limit at n=24, so this asks a differently-shaped question instead: does conditioning a prediction interval for pixel_error on the candidate narrow it versus a proxy-free baseline (predict the marginal mean)? CV+ (Barber et al. 2021, leave-one-clip-out folds — not literal functional conformal prediction, which is for curve-valued data; this is scalar per (clip,k)) at nominal 90% coverage: five of six candidates give wider intervals than the proxy-free baseline (+0.1% to +4.9%); the sixth is 1.3% narrower — noise. None help. (scripts/conformal_calibration.py)

3. Lead time. 8 of 24 clips crashed (excess-over-floor over threshold). The entropy-rate metric gave early-warning lead time on 0 of them; Wilson 95% CI on that rate is [0%, 32%]. (scripts/experiment3_lead_time.py)

4. Sign stability. Entropy vs. perturbation-strength slope is negative and significant under a raw rollout (95% CI [−0.0075, −0.0039], r² up to 0.97); under a context-corrected rollout the slope trends positive and is not itself significant (95% CI [−0.0023, 0.0189]), but its interval is fully disjoint from the raw-rollout one — the two modes are statistically distinguishable from each other, so correction isn't just adding noise, it's changing the metric's relationship to the perturbation it's meant to track. (scripts/experiment4_sweep.py, scripts/experiment5_interactions.py)

6. Ranking consistency. Across 96 conditions and 4,026 decided pairwise comparisons, only 678 (17%) agree with a global Copeland ranking; 10 explicit 3-cycles found — a formal certificate of intransitivity from the observed data, not a sampling estimate: no scalar ordering fits. (scripts/experiment6_transitivity.py)

None of these are implementation bugs to fix — Stage E already ruled the decoder out as the source of signal, and EBID is a reasonable transplant (an entropy-rate crash detector that works for ecological population collapse). The result is the finding: for this frozen latent-only predictor, pixel-space divergence isn't something these latent-space proxies can stand in for. See the rollout gallery for what this looks like clip by clip.

11.Mathematical interpretation

Everything above is definition. This section is the part that is a reading of the numbers — stated so the reading can be checked against the arithmetic.

11.1  Three independent formalisations of "the proxies don't work"

Each asks a differently-shaped question, so they fail in different ways and don't inherit each other's weaknesses.

(a) Correlation, with the power caveat stated rather than hidden.

$$\hat r(g) = \operatorname{corr}\big(g,\ \varepsilon\big) \quad\text{over all }(\text{clip},k)\text{ rows}, \qquad \text{CI}_{95}\ \text{by cluster bootstrap resampling } \textit{clips}\text{, not rows}$$
rows within a clip share one latent rollout — they are not independent, so the resampling unit has to be the clip

Best point estimate $\hat r = 0.121$ (normalized L2); raw latent drift itself reaches $0.013$. All seven 95% CIs include zero. But at $n=24$ clips a correlation test has ~80% power only for $|r| \gtrsim 0.55$, so "CI includes zero" alone is weak evidence. The honest bound is the CI upper limit, read per candidate: $0.122$ (Mahalanobis, tightest) to $0.498$ (ensemble variance, loosest).

(b) Conformal interval efficiency — the same conclusion without depending on power.

Coverage is guaranteed by construction for any score, informative or not, so coverage cannot distinguish them. Width can. With leave-one-clip-out CV+ folds:

$$\hat C_\alpha^{g}(x) = \Big[\ q^-_{\alpha}\big\{\hat f_{-c(i)}(x) - R_i\big\},\ \ q^+_{\alpha}\big\{\hat f_{-c(i)}(x) + R_i\big\}\ \Big],\qquad R_i = \big|\,\varepsilon_i - \hat f_{-c(i)}(g_i)\,\big|$$ $$W(g) = \mathbb{E}\big[\,\big|\hat C^{g}_\alpha\big|\,\big], \qquad \textbf{g is informative}\iff W(g) < W_0,\quad W_0 = W(\text{const})$$
CV+ (Barber, Candès, Ramdas, Tibshirani 2021) guarantees marginal coverage ≥ 1 − 2α = 80% at α = 0.1; empirical baseline coverage 90.8%. Not functional conformal prediction — this data is scalar per (clip, k), so plain CV+ is the correctly-scoped tool.

Measured: five of seven scores give wider intervals than the proxy-free baseline ($+0.06\%$ to $+4.9\%$); normalized L2 is $1.3\%$ narrower. None tightens the interval meaningfully.

A consistency check between (a) and (b). In the Gaussian-linear idealisation, conditioning on a predictor with correlation $r$ shrinks the residual scale by $\sqrt{1-r^2}$, so an interval-width ratio $w$ implies

$$|r| \;\approx\; \sqrt{1 - w^2}\;:\qquad w = 0.987 \ \Rightarrow\ |r| \approx 0.16, \qquad \text{vs. measured } \hat r = 0.121 \ \Rightarrow\ w \approx 0.993$$

The two analyses land in the same place to within a factor the idealisation can't resolve. This bridge is an interpretive cross-check added here, not an output of either script — CV+ width is not the Gaussian oracle width, and the agreement is order-of-magnitude, not a test.

(c) Ordering — a certificate, not an estimate.

Over 96 conditions $(\text{mode}, \epsilon, j)$, define a majority (Condorcet) tournament on clips:

$$A \succ B \iff \#\big\{c : \varepsilon_c(A) < \varepsilon_c(B)\big\} \;>\; \#\big\{c : \varepsilon_c(B) < \varepsilon_c(A)\big\}$$ $$\text{If }\exists\, u:\ \text{conditions}\to\mathbb{R}\ \ \text{with}\ \ A \succ B \iff u(A) > u(B),\ \ \text{then } \succ \text{ is a strict total order, hence }\textbf{acyclic}.$$
10 explicit 3-cycles were found (A ≻ B ≻ C ≻ A). One such cycle refutes the existence of any scalar stability index u over these conditions — this is a proof by counterexample, not a significance test, so no sample-size caveat applies to the refutation itself.

The Copeland score $\operatorname{Cop}(A) = \#\{B : A \succ B\}$ gives the best available scalar summary; the total order it induces agrees with only $678 / 4026 = 16.8\%$ of decided pairs.

(d) Sign stability. The entropy-vs-perturbation slope is fit per pipeline mode. The two CIs are disjoint and straddle zero:

$$\beta_{\text{pure}} \in [-0.0075,\, -0.0039], \qquad \beta_{\text{corrected}} \in [-0.0023,\, +0.0189]$$
the metric's relationship to perturbation strength depends on a design choice orthogonal to the instability it claims to measure

What these four together do and do not license. They say: for this frozen latent-only predictor, no tested latent-space scalar substitutes for decoding against ground truth. They do not say a latent-only proxy is impossible in general — (a) is underpowered, (b) tests only linear conditioning, and (c) refutes a total order over these conditions. That is exactly why the protocol's step 2 is "re-run this battery on your model", not "latent proxies don't work".

11.2  What $\rho$ actually measures, and why direction flips

$\rho = R[\varepsilon]/R[\delta]$ asks which of the two error coordinates carries the horizon dependence. Across three predictor families the ratio's bootstrap CIs are mutually disjoint and all exclude $1$:

Family$R[\delta]$$R[\varepsilon]$$\rho$95% CIon predicted side
A · V-JEPA2 (frozen, OOD query)2.0%12.0%6.10[2.03, 14.00]99.96%
B · DINOv2 + trained MLP34.3%9.9%0.29[0.20, 0.42]100%
C · action-free LeWM-style, from scratch66.0%4.1%0.06[0.026, 0.132]100%

The reading — and it is a reading — is that the direction tracks how the predictor relates to the query, not architecture. Both trained families minimise a latent-space objective of the form

$$\min_\phi\ \mathbb{E}\Big[\big\|P_\phi(Z_t,k) - Z^\star_k\big\|^2\Big] \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \delta_k \text{ inherits its horizon dependence directly from the training residual,}$$
while their decoders are trained only on real tubelets, so ε_k ≈ ε_floor + (small horizon term) — a large R[δ] against a small R[ε], i.e. ρ ≪ 1

V-JEPA2 is the opposite: queried far from its training mask distribution (S3), its output is nearly horizon-invariant in latent space — $R[\delta] = 2\%$ — which is the same insensitivity S9's contracting re-injection rate independently shows. Flat $\delta$ with moving $\varepsilon$ is precisely the configuration in which a latent-space check cannot see what pixel space sees.

Note also that $\rho$ and the separation residual $r$ are different axes: Family C shares B's direction ($\rho \ll 1$) but has a residual close to V-JEPA2's ($0.0131$ vs $0.0099$, against B's $0.0004$). A proxy validated on B's residual would not have been safe on C.

11.3  The floor is bookkeeping, not an exact decomposition

$\operatorname{excess}_k = \varepsilon_k - \varepsilon_{\text{floor}}$ is used to separate rollout-attributable error from the decoder's own reconstruction limit. This is a first-order convention, stated as such: the two error sources are not independent and do not add in quadrature, so

$$\varepsilon_k \;\ne\; \sqrt{\varepsilon_{\text{floor}}^2 + \varepsilon_{\text{rollout}}^2}\ \ \text{in general};\qquad \operatorname{excess}_k \ \text{is a }\textit{comparable}\text{ quantity across } k\text{, not an identified component.}$$

It is used only in ways that survive that: as a threshold for "crash" in the lead-time test, and as a horizon-varying quantity compared against itself.

11.4  Decoder capacity as an epistemic constraint

The claim "a stronger decoder would hallucinate detail the latent doesn't carry" can be made precise rather than asserted. From S5, patch $j$ of the decoded image is a function of token $j$ alone:

$$\hat x^{(j)} \ \text{is} \ \sigma\big(\hat z_j\big)\text{-measurable}, \qquad \mathcal{H} = \Big\{\ \hat z \mapsto \sigma\big(W\hat z + b\big)\ :\ W \in \mathbb{R}^{1536\times 1024}\ \Big\}$$
a 1024-dimensional affine family per patch, ≈1.57M parameters shared across all 256 tokens, with no cross-token attention, no learned image prior, no adversarial or diffusion objective

So any structure visible in $\hat x$ beyond a per-patch affine image of its own token must have come from $\hat Z$. Contrast a diffusion decoder, whose output distribution is dominated by a learned prior $p_{\text{prior}}(x)$ and which can therefore produce a sharp, plausible frame from a latent that carries very little — exactly the failure mode this protocol exists to detect. The trade is explicit: decode quality is given up to buy decode honesty, and $\varepsilon_{\text{floor}}$ is the price, measured rather than assumed.

12.Protocol (adopt this)

The reusable output of Section 10 isn't "these six metrics failed" in isolation — it's a checklist for evaluating any frozen, latent-only state predictor with no native decoder. Skipping straight to step 3 without 1–2 is how a decoder artifact gets reported as a predictor finding, or an untested latent-space proxy gets trusted anyway.

  1. Decoder-honesty gate first. Train the decoder only on real tubelets, never on rollouts, and pass the leakage + snapping checks before trusting anything downstream. Failing either means every pixel-space number below is measuring the decoder, not the predictor.
  2. Don't substitute a latent-space proxy for step 3. All six tested here failed on this predictor — treat any latent-only "confidence" signal as unverified on your model until it passes the same battery (Experiments 1, 3, 4, 6).
  3. Decode and compare directly. The only check here that reliably surfaces the divergence a latent-only proxy misses.
  4. Check re-injection compounding separately. Distinguishes one-shot decode noise from rollout drift that compounds — steps 1–3 alone can't tell these apart.

Implemented against the Encoder/Decoder/StatePredictor protocols in latent2rgb/interfaces.py, not against V-JEPA2 specifically — any model satisfying those interfaces runs the same four steps unmodified.

13.Two more model families: does the gap track V-JEPA2, or the querying regime?

Everything above characterizes one frozen predictor queried causally, out-of-distribution. To find out whether the measurement gap is a property of that regime or of latent-only prediction generally, the identical protocol (Stage D + Experiment 1) was run against two more families, differing from V-JEPA2 (and from each other) in what's frozen vs. trained and in encoder architecture: Family B — a frozen, independently-pretrained DINOv2 ViT-S/14 encoder + a small MLP predictor trained here (latent2rgb/dinov2_adapter.py, delta_predictor.py); and Family C — an action-free variant of LeWorldModel (Maes et al., arXiv 2603.19312): encoder and predictor trained jointly, end-to-end, from raw pixels, no pretraining at all (ViT-Tiny encoder + causal transformer predictor + SIGReg anti-collapse regularizer, latent2rgb/lewm_adapter.py, lewm_predictor.py). See Limitations for what "action-free" changes and what these families are/aren't evidence for.

Direction inverts on both, bootstrap-confirmed on both. latent_drift moves far more than pixel_error on both trained families (Family B: 34% vs. 10% range across k=2→32; Family C: 66% vs. 4%) — the opposite of V-JEPA2 (~2% vs. ~12%). Divergence-ratio 95% CIs (clip-level bootstrap): [2.0, 14.0] V-JEPA2, [0.20, 0.41] Family B, [0.026, 0.131] Family C — all three disjoint from each other and from 1.0; 100% (C) / 99.97% (V-JEPA2) of resamples land on the predicted side. Two independently-built, differently-trained predictors landing on the same side against V-JEPA2 is a real pattern. (stage_d_results_second_family.csv, stage_d_results_lewm_family.csv)

But separation residuals don't move together. Family B's residual is ~30x smaller than V-JEPA2's (0.0004 vs. 0.0099) — pixel_error nearly fully explained by the low-k latent_drift trend. Family C's residual (0.0131) is close to V-JEPA2's despite sharing Family B's direction — residual size and divergence direction are different axes; a proxy safe on B's residual wouldn't have been safe on C's.

Candidate-proxy correlations stay weak on Family C, unlike B. Best |r| = 0.20 (ensemble variance) at n=9 clips — closer to V-JEPA2's weak correlations than Family B's elevated (still not significant) r=0.39. (outputs/experiment1_lewm_family_summary.json)

Collapse checked, not assumed. Family C's encoder trains from scratch; SIGReg exists specifically to stop it collapsing to a constant output, which would trivially minimize prediction loss too and look identical to "good coupling." Checked directly: per-dimension embedding std ≈ 1.0 across all 192 dims (0.83–1.20) on 50 held-out tubelets from 10 clips — matching SIGReg's isotropic-Gaussian target, not a collapsed one.

Reading: divergence direction tracks how the predictor relates to its query — frozen-and-queried-out-of-distribution (V-JEPA2) vs. trained-toward-what's-being-measured (Families B and C) — not a fixed property of any one architecture. More specific and better-supported than "the gap is V-JEPA2-specific," and only visible because two, not one, differently-built families were run through the same protocol. See Limitations for what this result is and isn't evidence for.

Family C's training objective (SIGReg)

Family C trains encoder and predictor jointly from raw pixels with no pretrained representation, so it needs an explicit anti-collapse term — a constant encoder would trivially minimise the prediction loss and would look identical to perfect coupling in every number above.

$$\mathcal{L} \;=\; \underbrace{\big\| \hat Z_{1:T} - Z_{2:T+1} \big\|^2_{\text{MSE}}}_{\text{teacher-forced next-step prediction}} \;+\; \lambda\,\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{M}\textstyle\sum_{m} T^{(m)}}_{\text{SIGReg}}, \qquad \lambda = 0.1$$ $$h^{(m)} = Z u^{(m)},\quad u^{(m)}\sim \operatorname{Unif}\big(S^{D-1}\big), \qquad T^{(m)} = \int w(t)\,\Big|\varphi_N\big(t; h^{(m)}\big) - \varphi_0(t)\Big|^2 \, dt$$ $$\varphi_N(t;h) = \tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_n e^{\,i t h_n}, \qquad \varphi_0(t) = e^{-t^2/2}, \qquad w(t) = e^{-t^2/(2\lambda_w^2)},\ \lambda_w = 1$$
Epps–Pulley normality test on each 1-D projection. By the Cramér–Wold theorem, matching every 1-D marginal to a standard Gaussian implies matching the full joint — which is what makes M random directions a sufficient device. Quadrature: trapezoid, 17 nodes on t ∈ [0.2, 4], doubled by evenness of the integrand; t = 0 contributes nothing since φ_N(0) = φ₀(0) = 1.

Collapse was checked directly rather than assumed: per-dimension embedding std $\approx 1.0$ across all 192 dims (range $0.83$–$1.20$) on 50 held-out tubelets from 10 clips — matching SIGReg's isotropic-Gaussian target. At batch size 16 the per-batch normality estimate is noisier than the paper's 128; the direct check is what the claim rests on.

14.Limitations

  • Counterfactual faithfulness is untested. Every claim here is about whether latent-space observables predict pixel-space divergence under a fixed, non-action-conditioned rollout — none of it says whether the predictor responds correctly to different actions. That needs V-JEPA2-AC plus a real action-conditioned dataset (e.g. DROID, Bridge); both are far outside this project's disk budget (the existing V-JEPA2 checkpoint alone is 4.8GB against ~18GB free, those datasets run 100GB+). A stated scope boundary, not an oversight.
  • Both extra model families (Section 13) are lightweight stand-ins, not second SOTA world models. DINOv2 (Family B) was never pretrained for temporal dynamics; its predictor is a small MLP trained on ~26 clips for 40 epochs. LeWM-style (Family C) is an action-free variant of LeWorldModel, not a reimplementation — the paper's AdaLN action pathway is simply absent, since SSv2/Kinetics-mini carry no action labels. It's also trained at much smaller scale than the paper (batch size 16 vs. 128; 300 steps on ~26 clips vs. 10 epochs on 10,000-episode datasets) — SIGReg's per-batch normality estimate is noisier at this scale, though the direct collapse check in Section 13 passed. Neither family is independently pretrained, state-of-the-art, or a contribution in itself. They answer "does the gap reproduce on differently architected, differently trained latent-only predictors," not "does the gap hold for latent-only video world models in general" — that broader claim needs an independently pretrained SOTA model at real scale.
  • Only Stage D and Experiment 1 ran on the two extra families, not the full battery. Experiments 3 (lead-time), 4/5 (sign-stability), and 6 (transitivity) only ran against V-JEPA2; the n=9–10 clips per extra family are too few for Experiment 6's pairwise-ranking design especially (V-JEPA2's version used 26 clips for 4,026 pairs). Whether the sign-flip and intransitivity findings also hold on Families B/C is untested, not confirmed.
  • Sample sizes are small, and "CI includes zero" isn't strong evidence alone at n=24. A correlation test at n=24 has ~80% power only for |r|≈0.55+. Mitigated two ways, not papered over: CI upper bounds are reported per-candidate (Section 10), and the conformal-efficiency check (1b, Section 10) corroborates the conclusion through a mechanism that isn't power-limited the same way. See outputs/stats_significance.json, experiment1_second_family_stats.json, and experiment1_lewm_family_summary.json for the bootstrap CIs and correlations behind every headline number in Sections 10 and 13.
